Gosho Aoyama Confirms He Has Already Drafted Detective Conan's Final Chapter Storyboard

More than 30 years into serialization, Detective Conan creator Gosho Aoyama reveals he has already drawn a draft storyboard for the manga's final chapter, though he hasn't revealed any details or when the series will actually end.

OtaHub Editorial · 2026-08-19 · 5 min read

In short: Gosho Aoyama, creator of Detective Conan, confirmed during a Japanese TV special that he has already drawn a draft storyboard for the manga's final chapter. He shared no plot details or timeline — the series is still serialized weekly in Shonen Sunday, with 108 volumes released as of April 2026.

Gosho Aoyama, the author of Detective Conan, has confirmed a piece of news that sent fans into a frenzy: he has already drawn a draft storyboard for the manga's final chapter. He shared the reveal while appearing on a special program ahead of Japan's 24-Hour Television charity broadcast.

Despite confirming the draft exists, Aoyama did not reveal any plot details, the identity of the mastermind behind the Black Organization, or a concrete timeline for when the series will actually end. He also mentioned once talking with One Piece creator Eiichiro Oda about how the two of them are among the few manga artists whose long-running works have also become long-running anime, still writing new material in parallel with the shows.

Having a storyboard in hand doesn't mean Detective Conan is about to wrap up anytime soon. The series has run continuously in Shogakukan's Weekly Shonen Sunday since January 1994, reaching 108 collected volumes as of April 2026, and remains one of the most commercially important properties for both the magazine and the publisher.

The news lands as Conan's annual theatrical films keep pulling in strong numbers at the Japanese box office every summer, a sign the franchise's pull hasn't slowed down even 33 years after its debut.
