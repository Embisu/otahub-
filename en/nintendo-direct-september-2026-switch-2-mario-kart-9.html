Nintendo Direct September 2026: Zelda 40th, Ocarina Remake

⚡ Key Nintendo Direct Highlights

Nintendo broadcast two presentations on consecutive days in September 2026. The first celebrated the 40th anniversary of The Legend of Zelda; the second focused on Nintendo Switch 2 games scheduled for the winter season. Together, the shows clarified the company's late-2026 lineup and gave Ocarina of Time its biggest spotlight in years.

The headline announcement was a rebuilt version of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for Nintendo Switch 2, scheduled to launch on November 5, 2026. Nintendo described it as a new-generation version of the 1998 adventure rather than a simple emulated rerelease.

September 8: Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct

The roughly 30-minute anniversary presentation covered games, hardware, music and live events. Series producer Eiji Aonuma presented gameplay from the new Ocarina of Time release and confirmed its November 5 launch date.

Nintendo says the Switch 2 version has fully overhauled visuals, voiced cutscenes, expanded character dialogue, symphonic orchestral music and revised camera and movement controls. It also uses the console's built-in microphone: when players hum or play one of Link's learned ocarina melodies near the system, Link can perform the corresponding song in the game.

The broadcast also introduced an anniversary-edition Nintendo Switch 2 system and accessories, a concert series and activities at the Nintendo Museum in Kyoto. Nintendo said the broader celebration would continue into 2027, so not every anniversary project is tied to the game's November release.

Why the Ocarina of Time remake is significant

Originally released for Nintendo 64 in 1998, Ocarina of Time established the targeting, camera and three-dimensional dungeon design that influenced later action-adventure games. It has been rereleased several times, including a stereoscopic 3D remake for Nintendo 3DS, but the Switch 2 project is positioned as a more extensive visual and presentation overhaul.

The new edition also gives Nintendo an accessible way to introduce the story to players who know Link and Zelda mainly through Breath of the Wild or Tears of the Kingdom. Its revised controls and navigation features are intended to reduce the friction of returning to a late-1990s design without replacing the original adventure.

September 9: the general Nintendo Direct

Nintendo's official Direct archive lists a second broadcast on September 9 at 7 a.m. Pacific time. The presentation ran for roughly 45 minutes and focused on Nintendo Switch 2 games launching during winter. Nintendo also released a longer Treehouse presentation with closer gameplay demonstrations after the main show.

The two broadcasts should not be confused. The September 8 show was dedicated to Zelda's anniversary, while the September 9 Direct covered the wider Switch 2 lineup. Viewers looking for a particular announcement can replay both presentations through Nintendo's official Direct archive.

What was corrected in this article

An earlier version of this page referred to “Mario Kart 9” and linked the program to a Switch 2 launch announcement. Those claims were not supported by Nintendo's official material and have been removed. Nintendo's current kart title is Mario Kart World; the September 2026 broadcasts described here took place after the Switch 2 had already launched.

For that reason, the article now separates confirmed announcements from earlier speculation and relies on Nintendo's own Direct archive and news release. Release dates can still change, so readers planning to buy Ocarina of Time should check the official product page before purchase.

Sources: Nintendo's Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct page, official anniversary news release and Nintendo Direct archive.
