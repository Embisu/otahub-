Kaiju No.8 Review: A Phenomenal Shounen Monster Hits

In-depth review of the Kaiju No. 8 manga: Naoya Matsumoto's monster-hunting blockbuster, Kafka's endearing grit, and its shonen impact.

Home › Manga › Kaiju No.8 Review: A Phenomenal Shounen Monster Hit
Reviews · Manga 2026-08-15 4 min read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Kaiju No. 8 by Naoya Matsumoto broke Shonen Jump+ records by spotlighting Kafka Hibino - a 32-year-old monster disposal worker pursuing his abandoned childhood dream. An in-depth review examining its refreshing adult perspective, explosive monster fights, and pacing dynamics.

Kaiju No. 8 builds on a compelling idea: a kaiju cleanup worker ingests a parasitic creature and can transform into a kaiju. Naoya Matsumoto sends Kafka Hibino into the Japan Anti-Kaiju Defense Force to fulfill a childhood promise to Mina Ashiro while hiding his monstrous form.

The manga ran on Shonen Jump+ from July 3, 2020 to July 18, 2025, in 16 volumes and over 19 million copies by September 2025.

Quick Facts

  • Author: Naoya Matsumoto
  • Publisher: Shueisha, Shonen Jump+ (July 3, 2020–July 18, 2025)
  • Scale: 16 volumes; over 19 million copies by September 2025
  • Awards: Next Manga Award 2021 (web), Tokyo New Manga Award, Geeks d'Ouro, Ayumi Comic Award; nominated for the 2022 Eisner and the Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize
  • Adaptations: Production I.G anime (season 1: April 2024, 12 episodes; season 2: July 2025, 11 episodes)

Story

Kafka Hibino is a kaiju cleanup worker who ingests a parasitic creature and can turn into a kaiju. He joins the Japan Anti-Kaiju Defense Force to keep a childhood promise to friend Mina Ashiro, now a captain, while concealing his monstrous form.

The story brings a protagonist in his thirties back to the battlefield, a difference from many shonen with youthful leads.

Behind The Scenes

Kaiju No. 8 was the best-selling new manga of 2020 within 28 days of volume 1's release and the fastest Shonen Jump+ series to reach 1 million copies. In France, volume 1 sold 22,041 copies in its first week, a manga debut record; in Italy, volume 1 sold 245,000 copies, surpassing Demon Slayer.

The series has repeatedly appeared on The New York Times bestseller lists since 2022. The Production I.G anime, supervised by Studio Khara, expanded the franchise.

Experience

Concept and kaiju design

Critics praised the wonderful premise, intricate kaiju designs, engaging characters and artistic quality. The protagonist being an adult with two sides, a monster and a defense worker, creates internal conflict for the story.

Pacing

Criticism centered on a formulaic opening, pacing that sacrifices character development and inconsistent art in some chapters. This is why some readers rate it lower than the idea's potential.

Anime adaptation

Production I.G produced the anime with season 1 from April 13 to June 29, 2024 and season 2 from July 19 to September 27, 2025. Anime News Network praised its wonderful and weighty beats, Digital Spy noted a lighter tone than Attack on Titan, and Polygon compared it to a mix of My Hero Academia and Pacific Rim.

Strengths

  • A compelling premise and intricate kaiju designs
  • An adult protagonist with internal conflict
  • Commercial success across many markets
  • Multiple awards including the 2021 Next Manga Award

Weaknesses

  • A formulaic opening
  • Pacing sacrifices character development
  • Inconsistent art in some chapters

What Critics Say

The manga earned praise for its premise, kaiju designs, characters and art, while criticized for a formulaic opening, pacing and inconsistent art quality. It won the Next Manga Award 2021, Tokyo New Manga Award, Geeks d'Ouro and Ayumi Comic Award, and was nominated for the 2022 Eisner and the Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize.

The anime earned nominations at the 9th Crunchyroll Anime Awards and won Japan Expo's Best Ending award.

Who Is It For

For fans of monster shonen and mature protagonists. If you prioritize consistent art or slow character development, think twice. The manga is complete, so it can be read in one go.

Verdict

Kaiju No. 8 is a worthwhile monster shonen with a strong idea and major commercial success, despite a formulaic opening and uneven art. OtaHub scores it 8.9/10.
