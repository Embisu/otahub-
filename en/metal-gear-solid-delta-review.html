Metal Gear Solid Δ: Snake Eater Review: The Remake is Faithful in Every Detail

Konami remakes the 2004 masterpiece with Unreal Engine 5, Metacritic 85. OtaHub comprehensively reviews the classic remake Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater.

OtaHub › Reviews › Metal Gear Solid Δ: Snake Eater
Reviews 2026-08-15 7 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Metal Gear Solid Δ: Snake Eater is a comprehensive remake of the 2004 masterpiece developed by Konami Digital Entertainment using Unreal Engine 5. Using Metacritic 85 and OpenCritic 86, this remake almost entirely recreates the original story and gameplay, adding only modern improvements to controls and visuals without changing the core gameplay structure.

Metal Gear Solid Δ: Snake Eater (the symbol Δ is pronounced \"Delta\") is Konami's ambitious project: to recreate the entirety of Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater (2004), often considered the best work in Hideo Kojima's legendary series, using a modern graphics engine, retaining the original script, voice acting, and level design, only changing the technical presentation.

Absolutely faithful to the original, its strengths and limitations

First, let's be clear: this is not a \"reimagining\" in the style of Resident Evil 2 Remake or Final Fantasy VII Remake. Metal Gear Solid Δ retains almost entirely the mission structure, character dialogue, and narrative pace of the 2004 version. For veteran players, this is a nostalgic "homecoming," with the Tselinoyarsk jungle, the index camo system, CQC melee combat, and the classic confrontation with The Boss still retaining their emotional weight.

Unreal Engine 5: visually transformed but still retains the soul

Technically, the leap to Unreal Engine 5 brings dense vegetation, dynamic real-time lighting, and highly detailed character models, a stark contrast to the original PS2 graphics. The camouflage system, the heart of Snake Eater's gameplay, retains its mechanics but greatly benefits from the much more realistic and vibrant environments, making the "immersion in nature" experience more convincing than ever.

Issue: Does the remake make the game easier than the original?

Some critics point out that modern control customizations (assisted aim, over-the-shoulder camera like MGSV) make the overall experience significantly more accessible than the "classic, demanding" difficulty of the original 2004 game. Players can choose "Legacy" control mode to retain the original feel, but the new default mode somewhat softens the game's characteristic survival challenge.

Metal Gear Solid Δ: Snake Eater is a fitting tribute to one of the greatest works in gaming history, albeit cautiously refraining from recklessly overhauling the gameplay like other remakes of the same era. For newcomers, it's the perfect way to experience Kojima's masterpiece; for veteran fans, it might evoke more nostalgia than surprise.
