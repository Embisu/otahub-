Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium In-Depth Review: XCOM-Style Tactics Meets 3D Waifu

Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium (GFL2) launched globally on December 3, 2024 and in Vietnam on August 27, 2026 through THS Game. Leaving the 2D chibi card style of the 2016 original behind, MICA Team's sequel is a turn-based 3D tactical RPG with an isometric view.

1. Story: A Bleak Post-Collapse Saga

The story is set ten years after the fall of the private military company Griffin & Kryuger. The Commander leads a squad of Tactical Dolls (T-Dolls), among them Groza, Nemesis, Tololo and Colphne, through contaminated zones. Cutscenes are fully voiced in Japanese, Chinese and Korean.

2. Tactical Depth

Combat is grid-based and unforgiving of positioning mistakes.

Cover and Flanking

  • Cover: reduces incoming damage by roughly 20–35% depending on the type of cover.
  • Stability: while above 0, a unit behind cover takes about 60% less damage on top of that; the combined reduction can reach 85–95%.
  • Flanking: moving around the edge of cover removes the enemy's cover bonus, the key to taking out snipers and heavy gunners.

Stability Break

Each attack drains Stability by a different amount. When Stability hits 0, the unit's armor collapses: it relies only on the cover's own reduction, and takes full damage from shots that bypass cover or from melee attacks.

4. Graphics and Dormitory

The cel-shaded 3D visuals with physically based materials are a highlight. The Dormitory offers free-camera interaction and affection events, but its interactions are shallow compared with the tactical gameplay.

5. Drawbacks

  • Heavy 3D graphics can make mobile devices run hot and drain the battery during long sessions.
  • Some strong T-Dolls depend on duplicate upgrades for extra actions or range.
  • Resource pressure grows in the later game.
