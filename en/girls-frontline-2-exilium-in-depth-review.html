Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium In-Depth Review

Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium review: MICA Team's XCOM-style turn-based tactics game with Boundary Push, Artifact Recovery and gacha; global launch December 2024 with over 2 million players on day one.

Home › Reviews › Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium In-Depth Review
Mobile / PC · Tactical RPG · Review 2026-09-19 4 min read OtaHub Editorial
Key Takeaway
Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium review: MICA Team's XCOM-style turn-based tactics game with Boundary Push, Artifact Recovery and gacha; global launch December 2024 with over 2 million players on day one.

Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium is a turn-based tactics game in the spirit of XCOM, dressed in anime T-Doll imagery and a post-apocalyptic setting. MICA Team (Sunborn) blends grid-based combat with real-time exploration and roguelike mechanics, creating a gacha game with denser systems than average.

The game launched in China on December 21, 2023, globally on December 3 and 5, 2024, and is planned for Vietnam through THS Game.

Quick Facts

  • Release: China December 21, 2023; global December 3 and 5, 2024; Vietnam via THS Game
  • Developer: MICA Team (Sunborn Network Technology); multiple regional publishers; Unity engine
  • Platforms: Windows, iOS, Android, HarmonyOS NEXT
  • Model: Free-to-play with T-Dolls obtained through gacha
  • Sales: 2 million players on day one of the global release; three-day revenue over RMB 100 million

Story

The story takes place ten years after the original. Players command android T-Dolls as bounty hunters in Collapse-contaminated zones, encountering a human seemingly immune to the radiation and uncovering the activities of the Paradeus cult.

The visuals combine realistic physically based rendering for environments with anime character aesthetics, which the developers describe as a blend of moe and realism.

Behind The Scenes

Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium was developed by MICA Team at Sunborn Network Technology on Unity, costing over RMB 100 million with each character averaging about six months to complete. Before launching in China, the game had over 4.74 million pre-registrations.

The Chinese version launched on December 21, 2023, with publishers such as Darkwinter (North America, Australia, Ireland) and HaoPlay (Europe, UK, Japan, Korea, Taiwan) releasing the global version in early December 2024, and THS Game publishing in Vietnam.

Experience

Grid-based tactical combat

Turn-based grid combat shares much with XCOM, where positioning, cover and action order decide outcomes. Players build teams of T-Dolls and use each character's abilities to take down targets.

Boundary Push and Artifact Recovery

Beyond combat, Boundary Push is a real-time dungeon traversal mode with a top-down third-person view, and Artifact Recovery adds roguelike mechanics. Social features include dormitories and the explorable Crew Deck base.

Gacha and launch

T-Dolls are obtained through gacha with standard and seasonal banners. The Chinese release had over 4.74 million pre-registrations, was praised for its animation, but early players found the learning curve steep. Development cost exceeded RMB 100 million, and each character averages around six months to complete.

Strengths

  • XCOM-style tactical combat with real depth
  • Multiple modes: grid, Boundary Push and roguelike
  • Animation and art direction are praised
  • A distinctive post-apocalyptic setting

Weaknesses

  • A steep learning curve early on
  • A gacha game with characters behind pulls
  • The Chinese release drew controversy when some players boycotted

What Critics Say

The global release drew 2 million players on day one, ranking third on Google Play and fifth on the App Store in South Korea. Three-day revenue exceeded RMB 100 million (about $13.7 million), with year-end revenue estimated around RMB 400 million (about $54.8 million).

The Chinese release also sparked a boycott movement among some players under the slogan "if there are males, I won't play it", an example of the game standing at the center of debates over its content.

Who Is It For

For players who like turn-based tactics, XCOM and gacha games with system depth. Those wanting light entertainment may find it heavy. Consider gacha spending if you want to own your favorite characters.

Verdict

Girls' Frontline 2: Exilium is a gacha tactics game with rare depth, strong in combat and art direction, weaker in learning curve and gacha cost. OtaHub scores it 8.5/10.
