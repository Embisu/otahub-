Chainsaw Man Manga Review: From Denji to Asa Mitaka

In-depth review of Chainsaw Man Part 2: Tatsuki Fujimoto's psychological, surrealist evolution through the eyes of Asa Mitaka and the War Devil Yoru.

Home › Manga › Chainsaw Man Manga Review: From Denji To Asa Mitaka
Reviews · Manga 2026-08-16 4 min read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Chainsaw Man Part 2 (Academy Saga) marks Tatsuki Fujimoto's bold shift in narrative focus from Denji to socially awkward high schooler Asa Mitaka and the War Devil Yoru. An in-depth review of its psychological adolescent themes, surrealist pacing, and how it continues to reinvent dark modern shonen.

Chainsaw Man is one of the most discussed shonen manga of the decade thanks to its daring storytelling. Tatsuki Fujimoto keeps his own style within Weekly Shonen Jump's familiar structure, blending supernatural action, dark humor and emotion.

Part 1 ran from December 3, 2018 to December 14, 2020, and Part 2 ran on Shonen Jump+ from July 13, 2022 to March 25, 2026, totaling 24 volumes and over 36 million copies as of June 2026.

Story

Part 1 follows Denji, an impoverished teenager who fuses with Pochita, the Chainsaw Devil, then joins the Public Safety Devil Hunters and discovers his handler Makima is the Control Devil. Part 2 centers on Asa Mitaka, a high school student possessed by Yoru, the War Devil, who is pursuing Chainsaw Man.

The two parts differ clearly in central character and setting, so readers need time to adjust when moving from Part 1 to Part 2.

Behind The Scenes

Fujimoto deliberately preserved his individual style while adopting Weekly Shonen Jump's typical narrative structure. He drew inspiration from The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974) and cited the final battle of Kizumonogatari Part 3 as a direct influence for Part 1's climax.

Per industry coverage, Part 2 ended at chapter 232. The manga was the best-selling series in North America in 2022, with eight volumes among the top 20 best-selling manga.

Experience

Art and staging

Fujimoto's art and page composition are cinematic, using contrast between humor and violence for emotional impact. Many action sequences are designed like film scenes, and the page layouts use empty space to create rhythm.

Violence and themes

James Beckett of Anime News Network gave volume one a B+, praising its eccentric blend of humor, violence and emotional depth. Many critics viewed the graphic violence as thematically appropriate rather than gratuitous, distinguishing the series from conventional shonen.

Part 2 and the ending

Part 2 shifts to a school setting with new characters and ran on Shonen Jump+ rather than Weekly Shonen Jump. It ended on March 25, 2026, closing a run spanning nearly eight years for the work.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Daring storytelling that blends dark humor and emotion
  • Violence placed in thematic context
  • Multiple awards: Shogakukan and three straight Harveys
  • Complete and readable in full

Weaknesses

  • Violence and an unpredictable tone are not for every reader
  • Part 2 changes the central character and setting from Part 1
  • A storytelling style that can be divisive

What Critics Say

Critics consistently praise the balance between supernatural action, dark humor and emotional depth. The series won the 66th Shogakukan Manga Award (2021), the Harvey Award for Best Manga three years running (2021–2023) and three Darumas at Japan Expo 2022 for drawing, screenplay and action.

In sales, the manga passed 36 million copies by June 2026 and was the best-selling manga series in North America in 2022.

Who Is It For

For readers who accept violence, unpredictability and an unusual tone. Anime viewers can read on through Part 1 and Part 2 for the complete story.
