Mafia: The Old Country Review: Return to Sicily

Mafia: The Old Country review: Hangar 13 returns to linear storytelling in 1900s Sicily at $50, Metacritic 73; a striking setting and story, but shallow, repetitive mechanics.

Home › Gaming › Mafia: The Old Country Review: Sicilian Roots
Reviews · Gaming 2026-08-15 4 min read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Mafia: The Old Country review: Hangar 13 returns to linear storytelling in 1900s Sicily at $50, Metacritic 73; a striking setting and story, but shallow, repetitive mechanics.

Mafia: The Old Country returns to the series' roots: a linear, cinematically staged story set in early 20th-century Sicily. Hangar 13 deliberately drops Mafia III's open-world approach to focus on character and plot, priced at $50.

The game launched on August 8, 2025 on PS5, Windows and Xbox Series X|S, earning a Metacritic score of 73 and 66% recommendation on OpenCritic, a divisive result.

Story

The story follows 17-year-old Enzo Favara from labor in a sulfur mine to involvement with the fictional Torrisi crime family. The plot spans 1904 to 1907 and ends with Enzo making his way to Empire Bay, the series' city.

The game has full dubbing in several languages, including Sicilian, but notably lacks Italian despite the setting.

Behind The Scenes

Hangar 13 deliberately returned to deep linear storytelling after Mafia III's open-world approach and priced the game at $50, lower than many AAA titles. The game uses Unreal Engine 5 and is dubbed in English, French, German, Spanish, Czech, Russian and Sicilian, but not Italian.

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said the game sold better than expected, and The New York Times named it to its top 10 games of 2025.

Experience

Linear gameplay

The game is a linear third-person experience of driving, knife combat, shooting and stealth, with no open world to explore. Hangar 13 deliberately returned to deep linear storytelling after Mafia III. This keeps the story tight but also exposes the limits of the mechanics.

Setting and presentation

Sicily is rendered in Unreal Engine 5 with standout countryside and sulfur mine scenes, and visuals are often praised. The New York Times included the game in its top 10 of 2025, and it received nominations for storytelling, music and art direction.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Story and characters are praised, and the Sicilian setting stands out
  • A return to linear storytelling with tight pacing
  • A reasonable $50 price for a campaign-driven experience
  • Named to The New York Times' top 10 of 2025

Weaknesses

  • Shallow mechanics and dated design, per many reviews
  • Repetitive combat and occasionally clichéd storytelling
  • No Italian dub despite the Sicilian setting

What Critics Say

IGN and Destructoid both gave 8/10, GameSpot 6/10 and PC Gamer 45/100. Praise centered on the story, characters, Sicilian visuals and the return to linear design; criticism focused on shallow mechanics, dated design, a static world, repetitive combat and clichéd storytelling. Metacritic 73 and OpenCritic 66% reflect the split.

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said the game sold better than expected, validating the cinematic direction.

Who Is It For

For those who enjoy linear, cinematic campaigns or are Mafia series fans. If you need deep combat mechanics or a rich open world, the game will struggle to deliver. The $50 price lowers the risk.
