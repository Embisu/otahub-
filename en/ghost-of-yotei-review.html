Ghost of Yōtei Review: Sucker Punch Surpasses Tsushima's Shadow

Sucker Punch delivers a samurai epic that's bigger, deeper, and more emotional than Ghost of Tsushima. OtaHub comprehensively reviews after completing Atsu's revenge journey through the land of Ezo.

OtaHub › Reviews › Ghost of Yōtei
Reviews 2026-08-15 9 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Ghost of Yōtei, a sequel unrelated to the plot of Ghost of Tsushima, proves that Sucker Punch was not "lucky" in the first part. With Atsu and his journey to hunt for the Yōtei Six, the studio retains its signature cinematic beauty but delves deeper into combat, open world and emotional depth.

Nearly 5 years after Ghost of Tsushima, Sucker Punch returns with a completely new story but in the same spirit: Ghost of Yōtei. This time there was no Jin Sakai, no Mongols, instead Atsu, a young woman who lost her family to the Yōtei Six bandits 16 years ago, now returns to the Ezo region (present-day Hokkaido) with a list of 6 names to pay.

Atsu: the main character has more depth than Jin

If Jin Sakai in Tsushima is a story of samurai honor torn between tradition and survival, Atsu offers a completely different perspective-rougher, more personal, and full of rage. Atsu is not a samurai; she is an outlaw who teaches herself to fight for revenge. The way the game allows players to choose the order in which they hunt down each member of the Yōtei Six-Snake, Demon, Fox, Spider, Dragon, and Lord Saito-provides a significant sense of freedom, while making each boss fight feel like an independent story with its own narrative and twists.

Ezo open world: more free, more diverse than Tsushima

In terms of the open world, Sucker Punch listened to feedback from Tsushima. Ezo is larger, with more diverse terrain, from snow-capped mountains and dense forests to coastal plains, and features a bounty system, the ability to find weapon masters to learn new skills, and the ability to set up camp anywhere, making the pace of exploration much more relaxed than the previous installment. However, most of the side activities still carry the familiar Ubisoft-style open-world formula that Tsushima used, so for players already familiar with it, the novelty won't come from the structure but from the storytelling.

Combat: diverse weapons, more decisive

Atsu possesses a more extensive arsenal than Jin, ranging from traditional katanas to long-range weapons like muskets, and even special tools that are gradually revealed throughout the game. Combat retains the cinematic "duel" spirit of Tsushima but adds more layers of strategy, forcing players to read and adapt to each opponent in Yōtei Six instead of spamming familiar combos.

Small minus point

Ghost of Yōtei is not perfect. For those who have plowed hundreds of hours of Tsushima, some open world mechanics still feel like repeating the old formula. The pace in the middle of the game sometimes slows down when the side quests are not really closely connected to the main story line.

Ghost of Yōtei proves Sucker Punch can expand on the Tsushima formula without losing its identity. Atsu is a memorable protagonist, Ezo is a breathtakingly beautiful world, and her revenge journey, while not entirely structurally innovative, is emotionally powerful enough to be one of the best games of the year.
