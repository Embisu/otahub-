Vinland Saga Manga Review: Closing after 20 years with a worthy ending

After 20 years, Thorfinn's journey from revenge to peace has come to an end, and it is one of the most worthy endings in modern manga history.

OtaHub › Reviews › Vinland Saga (Manga)
Reviews 2026-08-15 9 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Vinland Saga Makoto Yukimura's has officially concluded after 20 years of serialization, with the final volume released on September 25, 2025, and the final chapter published on July 25, 2025. From its initial serialization in Kodansha's Weekly Shounen Magazine to its transition to the seinen magazine Afternoon, the series followed Thorfinn Karlsefni Thórdarson from a bloodthirsty warrior seeking revenge to a man seeking peace, bringing to a close one of the greatest character journeys in manga history.

Rarely has any manga gone through such a journey of genre transformation and ideological depth Vinland Saga by Makoto Yukimura. Starting out as a bloody Viking revenge story, the series gradually transformed into a profound meditation on violence, war and the true meaning of peace, and now, after 20 years, that journey has officially ended.

From revenge to enlightenment

Thorfinn Karlsefni Thórdarson begins the story as a young warrior yearning to kill Askeladd to avenge his father. But Askeladd's death plunges Thorfinn into a profound existential crisis, turning the latter half of the series into his quest for a non-violent way of life, one of the most compelling character transformations in manga history.

20 year journey of serialization

First published in Kodansha's Weekly Shounen Magazine before moving to the seinen magazine Afternoon, Vinland Saga has accompanied readers for two decades, a period long enough to witness the remarkable maturation of Makoto Yukimura's drawing style and intellectual depth through each chapter. The final volume officially released on September 25, 2025, concluding the story with the final chapter published earlier on July 25, 2025.

The legacy of a seinen masterpiece

Vinland Saga is not only an excellent historical adventure manga but also a powerful anti-war manifesto, rarely expressed with such depth and consistency in a commercial work spanning two decades. The series' timely conclusion, without dragging on, further affirms Yukimura's solid artistic vision from beginning to end.
