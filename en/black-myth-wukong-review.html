Black Myth: Wukong Review: China's First Souls-like Masterpiece

Game Science turns Journey to the West into an epic, beautiful and challenging role-playing action journey, a historical milestone for the Chinese gaming industry, even though it is not technically perfect.

OtaHub › Reviews › Black Myth: Wukong
Reviews 2026-08-15 9 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Black Myth: Wukong, the first AAA game from Chinese studio Game Science, based on the classic novel Journey to the West, became a global phenomenon from its release, selling over 18 million copies in just the first three days. With action RPG gameplay inspired by the Souls-like genre, the game puts players in the role of the Mandate of Heaven on a journey to recover the legacy of Sun Wukong.

Rarely has any game in recent history created such an earthquake Black Myth: Wukong. As the first AAA project of Game Science, a Chinese studio previously almost unknown on the global gaming map, the game immediately broke numerous sales records upon its release, proving that the Chinese gaming industry can absolutely compete on equal footing at the global AAA level.

Journey to the West through a Souls-like lens

Inspired by the classic novel Journey to the West, the game puts players in the role of the Heavenly Mandate Person, a spiritual descendant of Sun Wukong, on a journey through the mythical lands of China to recover the six lost senses of the Great Sage Equal to Heaven. The combat system borrows many familiar elements from the Souls-like genre (difficult opponents, need to memorize patterns, severe punishment upon death) but adds the ability to transform into various creatures and a flexible staff system, creating a distinct combat identity that doesn't completely copy FromSoftware's formula.

Graphics and art design: world class

Visually, Black Myth: Wukong is among the most beautiful games ever produced, recreating Chinese mythology through majestic settings inspired by numerous real-life historical sites across China. The boss designs are diverse and impressive, with each boss battle featuring a unique story and visual style, reminiscent of the production quality of top-tier Japanese blockbusters.

Minus: optimization and repetitive structure

Despite its enthusiastic reception, the game faced some notable criticisms: unstable performance on some PC configurations at launch, requiring a fairly high-end setup for a smooth experience. The level structure also leaned heavily towards linear corridors leading to bosses rather than a true open world, causing some players to feel the pace was somewhat repetitive in the mid-game.

Despite some limitations in optimization and level structure, the game's visual appeal and cultural depth are enough to make it one of the most important gaming works of the 2020s.
