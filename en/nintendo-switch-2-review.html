Nintendo Switch 2 Review: Perfect or Just an Upgrade?

Nintendo Switch 2 officially launched with a 1080p screen, magnetic Joy-Con and an impressive library of launch titles. Comprehensive evaluation after 3.

Home › Reviews › Nintendo Switch 2 Review: Perfect or Just an Upgrade?
Reviews 2026-05-28 5 min read OtaHub Editorial
Tom Tat
Nintendo Switch 2 launches in March 2025. Price: 449 USD. Screen: 1080p OLED 7.9 inches. CPU/GPU: NVIDIA custom. RAM: 12GB. Magnetic Joy-Con (no slide required). Launch title: Mario Kart World.

After 8 years of dominating the hybrid gaming segment, the Nintendo Switch has reached its hardware limits. The Switch 2 isn't a question of "whether it's necessary," but rather whether Nintendo can repeat the magic of the first time. After 3 months of real-world use, the answer is: almost perfect.

Design: Familiar But Upgraded In The Right Places

Switch 2 looks like Switch 1 but larger, 7.9 inch OLED 1080p screen compared to the old 6.2 inch LCD. Newly used Joy-Con magnetic connection instead of sliding rails, no more drift (hopefully), tighter attachment and easier removal. The new C button on the right Joy-Con opens the "GameChat" feature, a video call built into the console, a strange feature but more useful than expected.

Performance: Finally 4K (When Docked)

The Switch 2's custom NVIDIA chip enables 4K output when docked with DLSS upscaling, and 1080p/60fps in handheld mode. This sounds simple but has great implications: games like Metroid Prime 4, Zelda Next and ports from PS5/Xbox all run stably and beautifully. 12GB RAM compared to the Switch 1's 4GB also means loading times are significantly shorter and the game is less limited in asset quality.

Launch Titles: Mario Kart World Takes the Lead

Mario Kart World is the main launch title and it really deserves that position. 32 original tracks, open world exploration between races, smooth multiplayer and a significant visual upgrade compared to MK8 Deluxe. There are also Donkey Kong Bananza (completely new), Kirby Air Riders reboot and a series of upgraded ports from Switch 1 with significant improvements.

Backward Compatibility: Almost Perfect

Most Switch 1 games run on Switch 2, and many games are automatically upscaled. Some titles receive free patches that increase resolution and framerate. This is where Nintendo gets it completely right: users don't feel like their old library is abandoned.

Score: 9.0/10, Best Console to Buy in 2025

The Nintendo Switch 2 doesn't have any major weaknesses, it was more expensive than the Switch 1 at launch, but for what it offers, the $449 price tag is reasonable. 9.0/10 for a console that evolves properly, keeping what made its predecessor great and fixing what needed fixing.
