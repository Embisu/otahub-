Kagurabachi Review: Manga Explodes the West Without Anime

Takeru Hokazono's sword revenge manga has surpassed 2.2 million copies in circulation and won the Next Manga Award without an anime adaptation. OtaHub reviews 8 released volumes.

Home › Manga › Kagurabachi Review: Manga Boom in the West
Reviews · Manga 2026-08-15 5 min read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Kagurabachi by Takeru Hokazono is a rare phenomenon: a Shounen Jump manga that conquered the West more strongly than its home country of Japan, without needing a single anime series for promotion. With over 2.2 million copies in circulation and the 10th Next Manga Award, it is one of the most impressive Jump newcomers of the decade.

Launching in Weekly Shounen Jump from September 2023, Kagurabachi became a topic of discussion among the global manga fan community after just the first chapter. To date, with 9 volumes released and about to be released and CygamesPictures confirming the anime adaptation, this is the right time to look back at why this series is so hot.

Plot: classic revenge, excellent execution

Chihiro Rokuhira is the son of Kunishige, the legendary swordsmith who created six magical swords that ended a war. After the war, he retrieved all six blades and hid them, but a group of mages raided his home, killing Kunishige right before Chihiro's eyes. Years later, Chihiro, armed with his father's seventh and final magical sword, sets out to recover the six stolen blades.

Why did it cause fever in the West before Japan?

What's special about Kagurabachi is its trajectory of dissemination. Unlike most hit manga that usually spread strongly in Japan first before "exporting" internationally, Kagurabachi exploded in the Western fan community first, largely thanks to the rapid translation speed of VIZ Media/Manga Plus and the extremely attractive visuals of its characters by Hokazono. Only when its online reach peak did the interest "reverse" back to the Japanese domestic market.

Strengths: characters and design

In terms of quality, Kagurabachi scores highest in character development and its dark moral depth, going beyond a simple good-evil story. Hokazono's character design, especially that of the main "Hishaku" antagonists, is highly praised for its level of detail and visual individuality, a crucial element that helped the series go viral on social media even among those who haven't read a single chapter.

Points to note

Not all feedback has been overwhelmingly positive. In the early stages, some readers mocked Kagurabachi's "revenge swordsmanship" premise as rather clichéd and overly clichéd, causing some to stop reading early. However, as the story progressed through the later chapters, much of the initial criticism subsided as the depth of the plot became clearer.

  • Author: Takeru Hokazono
  • Start posting: Weekly Shounen Jump, September 19, 2023
  • Circulation: more than 2.2 million copies (as of May 2025)
  • Prize: Next Manga Award 10th time
  • English publication: VIZ Media / Shounen Jump (Manga Plus)
  • Anime adaptation: CygamesPictures (announced December 2024)

Kagurabachi is proof that a familiar premise can still create a global phenomenon if executed well enough. With impressive character designs, increasing plot depth, and a promising young author, it's one of the most watchable Shounen Jump manga currently, and with the upcoming anime, its popularity is sure to increase even further.
