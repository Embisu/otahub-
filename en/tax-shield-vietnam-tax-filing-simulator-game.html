Tax Shield: A Free Vietnamese Tax Filing Simulator Game

A tax filing simulator just went free on Threads: fill in returns, check invoices, settle taxes and face down auditors under Vietnam’s 2026 tax laws. Its tagline says it all: “Filing taxes isn’t hard, it’s just easy to end up in jail.”

Gaming 2026-10-02 8 min read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Tax Shield – Tax Monster Hunt 2026 is a free browser game released by Threads user @youngader6868 and playable on phones. It has 16 modes, from VAT returns and the new five-bracket personal income tax to corporate tax settlement and responding to the tax office, wrapped in a story, a monster-hunting map and a leaderboard. The content follows Vietnam’s new tax laws for 2026, but the creator stresses it is a simulation: always check current regulations when filing for real.

“Filing taxes isn’t hard, it’s just easy to end up in jail.” That half-joking line is the tagline of Tax Shield – Tax Monster Hunt 2026, a tax filing simulator that Threads user @youngader6868 has just released for free. Instead of slaying monsters for gold, you fill in returns, check invoices, calculate payroll tax and face down auditors. For anyone heading into their first tax settlement season, it is a far less stressful rehearsal than the real thing.

What is Tax Shield?

Tax Shield is a browser game with nothing to install. In the Threads post, the creator said they were opening the game to everyone “thanks to the community’s contributions”, and that they had rebuilt the interface to work on phones. Players just open the link or scan the QR code attached to the post. The game is in Vietnamese and is built around Vietnam’s tax system.

The creator is also upfront that the game is “pretty heavy” on tax and audit knowledge, so working professionals and students in accounting, auditing and finance will do better. That is what sets Tax Shield apart from just-for-fun office simulators: every level is a real accounting problem, with an answer and an explanation that cites the legal basis.

The story: from nervous intern to tax director

You play a new accountant at Juventus Co., Ltd., which has just received an audit notice. Your companions are Boss Long, the director who hands out daily tasks, and Khiên Khiên, “the shield that protects the company from tax risk”. The villain is Inspector Tax Shadow, who warns that “one small error is enough for me to fine the whole company”.

The story runs across five chapters matching five ranks, from “Nervous Intern”, when the boss drops a stack of invoices on your desk on day one, to a final battle as tax director, defending the company against the Tax Shadow. There is also a daily task list that mirrors a real tax accountant’s job: closing the books, reconciling payables, checking e-invoices, opening the eTax inbox and logging response deadlines for tax office notices.

16 modes built around real tax work

Tax Shield’s biggest strength is how much ground it covers. Some highlights:

  • VAT return: sort invoices and calculate boxes [35], [25] and [40] on form 01/GTGT.
  • Personal income tax: calculate tax on wages using the new five-bracket schedule, including a multi-employee payroll version.
  • Corporate income tax settlement: strip out non-deductible expenses and calculate tax based on revenue.
  • Special consumption tax, foreign contractor tax, import duties and registration fees: from beer, alcohol and cars to CIF-based imports, foreign contractors, property and vehicles.
  • Household businesses and special PIT cases: revenue-based filing, property and securities transfers, inheritance and gifts.
  • Blitz: five questions, 15 seconds each, for a quick review.
  • Surprise audit and Responding to the tax office: decide how to handle an audit, prepare documents, answer officials and calculate what you owe.
  • A day as a tax accountant: an 8:30–17:30 shift that follows the real cycle: check documents, reconcile with the tax portal, prepare returns, file and pay, then respond to queries. It ends with a performance report.

On screens with number inputs, a floating tax calculator is built in, just like at a real desk.

Hunting “tax monsters” under jail-themed rules

To make tax less dry, the game turns each topic into a region on a map with its own monsters: the Invoice Swamp has the “Fake Invoice Zombie” and the “Wrong Tax ID Ghost”, the Payroll Valley has the “Deduction Wolf”, the Expense Cave has the “Phantom Expense Dragon”, and the Market Village has the “1-Billion Threshold Boar”. The last two regions are the Audit Fortress and the Explanation Hall.

The rules are where the “easy to end up in jail” joke really lands. You start with five reputation points. Each wrong answer or timeout costs one and moves you along a simulated penalty scale: reprimand, heavy fine, high risk, travel ban and finally prosecution. Three correct answers in a row restore a point, win streaks multiply rewards by up to 60%, and coins can be spent on hints. The final boss is the “Interrogation Room”: eight questions over three rounds about your books, invoices and statements, and it starts automatically if you rack up five violations.

How closely does it follow Vietnam’s 2026 tax laws?

The game cites a legal basis for every question. OtaHub checked the key milestones against the actual documents:

  • Personal Income Tax Law No. 109/2025/QH15 was passed by the National Assembly on December 10, 2025 and takes effect on July 1, 2026. The family deduction is 15.5 million VND per month for the taxpayer and 6.2 million VND per month for each dependant, and the progressive schedule shrinks from seven brackets to five. The wage provisions apply to the entire 2026 tax year, so they will show up in settlements in early 2027.
  • Tax Administration Law No. 108/2025/QH15 takes effect on July 1, 2026, while its rules on filing, tax calculation and e-invoices for household and individual businesses apply from January 1, 2026.
  • Circular 89/2026/TT-BTC, issued on June 30, 2026 and effective July 1, 2026, replaces the old set of tax forms. From then on, employers file personal income tax on wages quarterly, plus an annual settlement.
  • The game also covers familiar rules such as invoices of 5 million VND or more needing non-cash payment to claim VAT input credit, and late-payment interest of 0.03% per day.

The game itself reminds you on its first screen that the questions are simulations and that you should check current regulations when filing for real. Some figures, such as revenue thresholds for household businesses or exemption caps, are worth confirming with the tax office before applying them to your own filing.

Who should try Tax Shield?

  • Accounting, auditing and finance students: practice through scenarios instead of only reading textbooks.
  • New employees: the personal income tax mode explains how tax is withheld from wages, how deductions work and why year-end settlement matters.
  • Working accountants: Blitz and A day as a tax accountant are quick ways to catch up on the 2026 changes.
  • Small business owners: the household business mode gives a basic picture of revenue-based filing.

One small note: to play and save progress, you need to enter a display name, which may appear on the public leaderboard. A nickname is a better choice than your real name.

FAQ: Tax Shield

Where can you play Tax Shield, and is it free?

It runs directly in the browser at khai-bao-thue-stimulator.vercel.app, is free, and has been optimized for phones. You only need to enter a display name to start playing and join the leaderboard. The game is in Vietnamese.

Who is Tax Shield for?

According to its creator, the game is heavy on tax and audit knowledge, so it suits working professionals and students in accounting, auditing and finance best. New employees can also use the personal income tax mode to understand how tax on wages works.

Does the game follow the new tax laws?

It cites recent documents such as Personal Income Tax Law 109/2025, Tax Administration Law 108/2025 and Circular 89/2026/TT-BTC. Still, it is a simulation; when filing for real, check the current regulations or ask the tax office.

What is the family deduction in Vietnam for 2026?

Under Personal Income Tax Law No. 109/2025/QH15, the deduction is 15.5 million VND per month for the taxpayer and 6.2 million VND per month for each dependant, applied to the whole 2026 tax year.

Do you really “go to jail” if you lose in Tax Shield?

No, it is just a game mechanic. Each mistake costs one reputation point and moves you along a simulated penalty scale; run out of reputation and you face the “interrogation room”. As the game itself reminds you: file correctly, completely and on time, and you will be fine.

OtaHub’s take

Tax Shield is a fun example of the Vietnamese community using games to tackle a very everyday fear: filing taxes. It has no flashy graphics, but it has what many guides lack: concrete scenarios, instant feedback and enough humor to keep players going. For anyone settling taxes for the first time or studying for an accounting exam, a few rounds can make concepts like family deductions, input VAT and late-payment interest feel far less foreign. Just remember: the game is for practice, and your real filing should always follow current regulations.
