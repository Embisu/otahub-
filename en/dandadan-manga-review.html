Dandadan Review: A Whirlwind of Humor, Horror and Romance Like No Other

Yukinobu Tatsu mixes demons, aliens, raunchy humor and teen thrills into a formula unlike any other manga, and it works brilliantly.

OtaHub › Reviews › Dandadan
Reviews 2026-08-15 8 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Dandadan by Yukinobu Tatsu, former assistant to Tatsuki Fujimoto (Chainsaw Man), has become one of the biggest manga phenomena on Shounen Jump+, telling the story of Momo Ayase, a girl from a family of shamans, and Okarun, a fan of occultism, as they confront both ghosts and aliens after an argument about "which is real".

There is a seemingly silly question that underlies the whole thing Dandadan: Ghosts or aliens, which really exists? Momo Ayase believes in ghosts but denies aliens; Okarun is the opposite. To prove their point, they both embark on separate adventures, and both are right, in the worst way possible.

The formula is like no other: humor, horror, action, romance

What sets Dandadan apart from the plethora of manga of its time is its astonishingly smooth tone-shifting ability, a scene that can be both terrifying and absurdly funny within pages, before transitioning to a sweet romantic moment between Momo and Okarun. Yukinobu Tatsu handles this bold blend with rare precision, ensuring no element overwhelms or dilutes the others.

Monster design and action: legacy from Chainsaw Man

Experience as an assistant to Tatsuki Fujimoto on Fire Punch and Chainsaw Man left a distinct mark on the way Tatsu designed monsters and staged action sequences-creative, bizarre, and sometimes hauntingly terrifying, but always balanced by the series' signature comedic tone.

Momo and Okarun: chemistry is the heart of the series

Beneath the action-horror-comedy facade, Dandadan is actually a clumsy but sincere teenage love story. The relationship between Momo and Okarun develops naturally through each supernatural challenge they overcome together, bringing emotional depth that makes the series more than just a simple entertainment manga.
