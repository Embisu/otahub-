Re:Zero Season 4 Review: Memory, Loss and Return

Season 4 adapts all of Arc 6 in 19 episodes and turns the Pleiades Watchtower into a puzzle of memory and trust. The writing is dense and the animation steady, though it is hard going for anyone new to the series.

Home › Anime › Re:Zero Season 4 Review: Memory, Loss and Return
Anime 2026-10-03 9 min read OtaHub Editorial
Quick verdict
Re:Zero Season 4 is the most idea-rich and best-built adaptation the series has had in years, if you have followed it from the start. The Pleiades arc turns Subaru’s memory into the main source of tension, and the two-cour structure holds its emotional weight through a 45-minute episode 19. The price is a dialogue-heavy middle and almost no on-ramp for newcomers. OtaHub score: 8.8/10, Great.

Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World- Season 4 (Re:Zero kara Hajimeru Isekai Seikatsu) is made by White Fox, directed by Masahiro Shinohara and based on Tappei Nagatsuki’s light novels. It ran from April 8 to September 30, 2026, 19 episodes in all, and adapts Arc 6 at the Pleiades Watchtower. This Re:Zero Season 4 review scores the whole season now that all 19 episodes have aired, with no ending spoilers.

The season’s question is simple: when Subaru loses the memories that make him who he is, is “Return by Death” still a weapon? It takes two cours to answer, and most of the time that is more interesting than a big battle.

Story: Pleiades is a memory puzzle, not just a journey

Season 4 opens right after Season 3’s Priestella battle. Rem, Crusch and Julius are still suffering the effects of the Sin Archbishops, so the party crosses the Auguria Dunes to the Pleiades Watchtower to find Shaula, said to know everything. To meet her they must get through a series of “trials” set by the tower.

What makes the season work is how it puts Subaru in his weakest position. According to the official summary of episode 74, he enters the tower like someone just summoned, with no memory of his time in this world. Episode 76 pushes further: Subaru wakes beside Meily’s body with a message on his arm he doesn’t remember writing, then reads her “Book of the Dead” to trace her death. Seeing the death loop from an outsider’s angle is a good idea, because it lets the series talk about Subaru without repeating what viewers already know.

Loss Arc and Recapture Arc: two cours, two rhythms

The Loss Arc (season episodes 1 to 11) builds unease: memories vanish, allies doubt each other, the tower has its own rules. The Recapture Arc (episodes 12 to 19) is about winning things back: memories are pieced together, the party splits up duties and, per the episode 83 summary, five separate threats close in on the tower at once. The near two-month break between the parts interrupts the pacing, but the structure is clear enough that you won’t get lost.

One OtaHub observation: unlike Season 3, this season leans less on one huge climax and more on many small sequences, each carrying a question a character has to answer. That is why episode 19 doesn’t feel like fighting for the sake of it; it gathers threads laid long before.

Viewing experience: heavy and it asks you to remember

Re:Zero has always been hard to binge, and Season 4 pushes that further. Each episode carries plenty of terminology and many dialogue threads, and old relationships (Emilia, Ram, Beatrice, Julius, Anastasia) are used as emotional material. Newcomers will find it confusing; returning viewers will see every detail pay off.

What deserves credit is that the “die and retry” rhythm never becomes a free undo button. Episode 82, per the official summary, mentions fifteen deaths and memories piling up as books; that number reminds you each loop is a wound. The flip side is a middle stretch with a few explanation-heavy, action-light episodes, which tests weekly viewers more than people watching the whole season.

Visuals and sound: White Fox puts in the hours

Haruka Sagawa is both character designer and chief animation director, with two more chief animation directors credited alongside on many episodes. The key animator list for the finale runs past twenty names, a sign that effort was concentrated on the important episodes. The ice-magic scenes, Emilia’s attacks and Subaru’s anguished close-ups are what stay with you.

On sound, Kenichiro Suehiro composes and Jin Aketagawa is sound director. “Recollect” pairs Konomi Suzuki with Ashnikko, and MYTH & ROID’s “Ender Ember” with TK (Ling Tosite Sigure) carries the season’s dark tone into the ending. The cast stays steady: Yusuke Kobayashi (Subaru), Rie Takahashi (Emilia), Inori Minase (Rem), Rie Murakawa (Ram), Satomi Arai (Beatrice), Ai Fairouz (Shaula) and Tomokazu Sugita (Reid Astrea).

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • The amnesia premise explores “Return by Death” from a new angle and keeps emotion at the centre.
  • A two-cour structure with a clear destination; the 45-minute episode 19 gathers many threads.
  • Steady animation and direction, with visible investment in the key episodes.
  • Returning characters used in the right places; music and voice acting have their own character.

Weaknesses

  • Almost no room for anyone who hasn’t seen the previous three seasons.
  • The middle is heavy on dialogue and explanation, slow for weekly viewers.
  • The break between cours interrupts emotional momentum.
  • Many proper names and side threads packed into one tower.

What critics and viewers say

OtaHub found no aggregated critic score for Season 4 specifically, so what follows leans on audience ratings, read on October 3, 2026. On MyAnimeList Season 4 stands at 9.13 from 141,633 ratings, ranked 22nd. In Anime Corner’s Spring 2026 awards the first part won 19 of 29 categories, including Anime of the Season, while best animation went to Witch Hat Atelier. Anime Corner’s weekly summer rankings also put Re:Zero first several times, for example week 9 (September 4) with 16.39% of the vote.

OtaHub’s 8.8 sits below the audience score. That isn’t because the season is weak: OtaHub docks the Experience criterion for its heaviness and its barrier to newcomers, things fan ratings tend to ignore.

Who should watch Re:Zero Season 4?

Watch it if you have seen Seasons 1 to 3 and like fantasy that weighs psychological consequences as heavily as action. Every returning detail gets paid off.

Think twice if you are new to Re:Zero or dislike layered storytelling. Start from Season 1 rather than jumping into Arc 6; this adaptation isn’t written to guide strangers in.

Frequently asked questions

How many episodes does Re:Zero Season 4 have, and when did it air?

Season 4 has 19 episodes in two parts: the Loss Arc (11 episodes, April 8 to June 17, 2026) and the Recapture Arc (8 episodes, August 12 to September 30, 2026). Episode 19 ran 45 minutes and has now aired in Japan.

Which part of the light novels does Season 4 adapt?

Season 4 adapts Arc 6, the Pleiades Watchtower story, which Wikipedia lists as volumes 21 to 25 of the light novels. As of October 3, 2026, OtaHub has seen no official announcement of a Season 5.

Where can I watch Re:Zero Season 4?

In Japan it aired first on AT-X and Tokyo MX, with early streams on ABEMA and d Anime Store. Internationally it is on Crunchyroll; availability varies by region, so check the legal services where you live.

Do I need to watch the earlier seasons first?

Yes. Season 4 picks up straight after the Priestella battle of Season 3 and leans heavily on the bonds between Subaru, Emilia, Rem, Ram, Beatrice and Julius. Watch at least Season 3 before starting.

What score does Re:Zero Season 4 get?

OtaHub scores it 8.8/10. On MyAnimeList it stood at 9.13 from 141,633 ratings (read October 3, 2026), ranked 22nd on the site at that point.
