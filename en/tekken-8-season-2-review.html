Tekken 8 Season 2 Review: A Costly Misstep

Season 2 brought Anna Williams, a reworked ranked system and four DLC fighters, but the Ver. 2.00 balance pass tilted hard toward offense, flooding Steam with negative reviews and driving players away. Bandai Namco had to apologise and patch in a hurry.

Home › Gaming › Tekken 8 Season 2 Review: A Costly Misstep
Gaming 2026-10-03 8 min read OtaHub Editorial
Quick verdict
Tekken 8 Season 2 has worthwhile new fighters, but its launch balance pass undermined the experience. Ver. 2.00 leaned heavily toward offense, the opposite of the promised defensive boost, so Steam filled with negative reviews and the player count sank. Later patches fixed a good deal, but the first impression stuck. OtaHub score: 4.5/10, Below Average.

Tekken 8 Season 2 is the second content season of Bandai Namco Studios’ 3D fighting game, released on March 31, 2025 on PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. It includes four DLC fighters, two new stages, a rebuilt ranked system and balance changes for all 32 characters. This review covers the Season 2 update, not the base game. The base game earned a 90/100 Metacritic score, while Season 2 was described by Dexerto as the worst review bomb a fighting game has ever had.

The disappointment has a very specific cause. Bandai Namco promised a stronger defensive game, yet Ver. 2.00 pushed play toward offense. The developers then had to apologise, ship an emergency patch and swap their balance team.

What Season 2 adds: fighters, stages and ranked

On content, the roadmap Bandai Namco announced on February 25, 2025 looked solid. Anna Williams returns with a rocket launcher and a new fighting style; Fahkumram and Armor King are returning faces; Miary Zo is a brand-new character and the game’s first original DLC fighter. The two paid stages are PAC-PIXELS (a Pac-Man theme) and BAOBAB HORIZON.

Ranked was rebuilt: the leaderboards reset, eight new top ranks arrived and the Tekken Power matchmaking was dropped. The update also added practice tools and an in-match display of combo count and combo damage. These are good changes, but they were overshadowed because the most important part of a fighting game update, balance, went off course.

The Ver. 2.00 balance pass: a defensive promise, an offensive reality

Before launch, the team said Season 2 would improve defense. Ver. 2.00 did the opposite. Every character gained new moves, Heat gained more offensive options, several new tracking moves made sidestepping less effective, and according to Dexerto the update held more than 1,500 changes. Players ran into strings that were hard to block, constant pressure and few openings to counter.

Heat and defense

Heat is the Tekken 8 mechanic that rewards sustained aggression. The emergency patch numbers show where things were pushed too far: Heat Engager frame advantage sat at +17, chip damage when blocking Heat Dash was 7, and combo damage inflated during Heat. Later patches mostly targeted these exact points. That is what hurt most: it was not a minor bug but the design direction of the whole season.

The characters in the spotlight

Dexerto named Paul and Jack for hard-to-block strings, while MP1st noted that Bryan Fury was clearly strengthened. According to Dexerto, some professional players even threatened to leave competitive play, and ranked players kept meeting abnormally strong characters. For a game that lives on competition, those reactions weigh heavily.

Bandai Namco’s response: from explanation to apology

At first Harada described the outcome as a gap between what the community wanted and the tuning results. After a few weeks of unrelenting backlash, game director Kohei Ikeda apologised publicly, saying the update had not met expectations and promising to win back trust through steady patches.

Emergency patch Ver. 2.00.02 arrived on April 17, 2025: health rose from 180 to 200, Heat Engager frame advantage fell from +17 to +9, chip damage on blocked Heat Dash dropped from 7 to 2, and many characters were adjusted, including Anna, King, Paul and Lars. In early May 2025 Harada confirmed that the balance team had been swapped back to the original game’s members, with Ikeda (Nakatsu) approving every change directly. A May patch addressed offense, June 3 focused on defense, and July 8, 2025 brought the final major Season 2 balance patch.

Players left and Steam filled with negative reviews

The numbers say it plainly. According to SteamCharts, the April 2025 peak was 16,091 after an initial surge at launch. But the monthly average was only 5,518, and esports.gg reported the 24-hour peak sliding to 6,040 on April 14; TheGamer put it below 6,000, the first time since launch. The all-time peak is 46,139 from January 2024. By September 2026 the monthly average was about 5,054.

On reviews, from launch to March 30, 2025 Steam had 47,251 reviews at roughly 64% positive. March 31 to April 30, 2025 alone brought 8,569 reviews with only 15% positive. Thanks to the patches, the last 30 days are up to 70.7% (607 reviews), though the overall score still sits at Mixed.

One point of fairness: the Tekken 8 foundation is still good. The base game’s combat system, roster, effects and presentation earned broad praise, and Season 2 shares that visual quality. The season’s problem lies in its balance design, not its graphics or sound.

Visuals, sound and lasting value

Visually, Season 2 changes little from the base game: detailed character models, clear hit effects and interactive stages. Anna and Armor King are carefully built, and the PAC-PIXELS and BAOBAB HORIZON stages look quite different from earlier arenas. On lasting value, a fighting game lives on its community, and Season 2 pushed the player count to its lowest point since launch. Season 3 arrived on March 18, 2026 promising a return to basics, but it was criticised for not reversing enough of Season 2’s changes.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Anna Williams, Armor King and Miary Zo bring new playstyles; Miary Zo is the game’s first original DLC fighter.
  • The reworked ranked system adds higher ranks and fairer matchmaking.
  • Later patches improved things substantially; the last 30 days on Steam sit at 70.7% positive.
  • The Tekken 8 foundation remains strong in visuals and combat feel.

Weaknesses

  • The Ver. 2.00 balance pass leaned toward offense, against the promised defensive boost.
  • An emergency patch and a swapped balance team show a testing problem.
  • The Steam player count dropped sharply, with the 24-hour peak around 6,000.
  • New fighters sit behind paid DLC while the balance was poor.

What critics say

There is no critic aggregate for Season 2 on its own. Metacritic scores only the base game, 90/100 from 96 reviews. Outlets such as PC Gamer, Dexerto, TheGamer and esports.gg reported on player reaction rather than scoring the update, and all described it as controversial. In an August 2025 interview with IGN, Harada admitted the team went too far in one direction, while Ikeda said he regretted that the changes did not meet expectations.

OtaHub’s view: the mistake was not the willingness to experiment but how it was done. A fighting game needs time for competitive players to test changes first, especially as Tekken 8 has no arcade release to serve as a trial run like earlier entries. The result: players became the testers.

Who should play Tekken 8 Season 2?

Consider it if you love Anna, Armor King or Fahkumram and want new fighters in your collection; the balance update is free, so you only pay for the characters. Newcomers can skip the Season 2 pass at first and focus on the base game.

Wait if you want a balanced, stable season for ranked play. The patches fixed a lot, but the launch is a lesson for both Bandai Namco and DLC buyers.

FAQ

When did Tekken 8 Season 2 release and what is new?

The Ver. 2.00 update launched on March 31, 2025 on PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, with Anna Williams (early access from March 31, general release April 3, 2025). The season adds four DLC fighters, two stages, a new ranked system and balance changes for all 32 characters.

Why was Tekken 8 Season 2 review-bombed?

Players felt Ver. 2.00 tilted too far toward offense, with new hard-to-block strings, powerful Heat and weakened defense, against earlier promises. Bandai Namco itself admitted the balance leaned too heavily toward offense and apologised.

Has Tekken 8 Season 2 been fixed?

Partly. Emergency patch Ver. 2.00.02 on April 17, 2025 raised health from 180 to 200 and cut Heat strength, followed by balance patches in May, on June 3 and on July 8, 2025. The balance team was also swapped back to the original game’s staff.

Should I buy Tekken 8 and the Season 2 Pass now?

The base game still holds a 90/100 Metacritic score and is discounted often. The Season 2 Pass only makes sense if you want Anna, Armor King, Fahkumram or Miary Zo; the balance update itself is free for every player.

Is Tekken 8 Season 3 any better?

Season 3 launched on March 18, 2026 promising a return to basics, but it was criticised for not reversing enough of Season 2’s changes and drew a fresh wave of negative Steam reviews. Emergency patch 3.00.01 followed on March 25, 2026.
