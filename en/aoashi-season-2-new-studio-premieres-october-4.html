Aoashi Season 2 Premieres October 4 With New Studio TMS

Aoashi season 2 officially premieres on October 4, 2026, moving production from Production I.G to TMS Entertainment, with a first cour of 12 episodes out of a planned 24.

Home › Anime › Aoashi Season 2 Premieres October 4 With New Studio TMS
Anime 2026-09-28 3 min read OtaHub Editorial
Key Takeaway
Aoashi season 2 officially premieres on October 4, 2026, moving production from Production I.G to TMS Entertainment, with a first cour of 12 episodes out of a planned 24.

Aoashi season 2 begins airing on October 4, 2026 on NHK E-Tele, bringing Ashito Aoi into the A team of Tokyo City Esperion. The new season has 24 episodes; the first cour has 12 episodes and airs at 5:00 p.m. every Sunday in Japan.

Schedule and main staff

  • Premiere: October 4, 2026, 5:00 p.m. Sunday on NHK E-Tele.
  • Episodes: 24; the first cour has 12 episodes.
  • Studio: TMS Entertainment, replacing Production I.G from season 1.
  • Director: Kazuki Yokoyama.
  • Series scripts: Masahiro Yokotani returns.
  • Character design: Manabu Nakatake, Asami Taguchi and Junko Yamanaka.

Crunchyroll has scheduled season 2 in many regions, including Southeast Asia. Release times may differ from the NHK broadcast slot, so viewers in Vietnam should check the licensed service directly.

What is Aoashi about, and why watch it even if you don't know soccer?

Aoashi is adapted from the manga of the same name by Yugo Kobayashi. It follows Ashito Aoi, a small-town striker who scores well but lacks tactical discipline, when the professional academy Esperion recruits him directly after an unexpected play catches the eye of coach Fukuda. What sets Aoashi apart from many soccer anime is that the story has almost no supernatural "special moves"; everything revolves around positioning, reading the game and communication between players, which makes the series one of the manga that depict real soccer tactics most realistically today, even for people who don't regularly follow soccer.

How far has Ashito come?

After overcoming many limits on the B team, Ashito is promoted to the A team, which gathers Esperion's strongest young players. There, his advantage of seeing the whole pitch is still useful, but the processing speed, physicality and tactical reading of his teammates are all at a higher level.

Season 2 opens the A team part and brings the story to the U-18 Premier League, a tournament that brings together the top academies. Ashito no longer only needs to prove he deserves to stay; he must learn to contribute to a complex tactical system and compete for a place against more experienced players.

What does the studio change mean?

TMS Entertainment takes over animation from Production I.G, while some of the first season's creative staff return. This combination gives the new season its own direction without breaking completely from the earlier storytelling style. The most important thing to watch is how clearly tactical movement reads: player positions, gaps and the rhythm of ball circulation need to be easy to follow, not just look good in the sprints.

Opening and ending themes

10-FEET performs the opening theme Fortress Defense; Ai Higuchi performs the ending theme Triangle. The first-cour trailer uses both songs to stress the pressure of competition and Ashito's process of finding his place in the A team.

Do you need to watch season 1?

Yes. Season 2 continues directly from Ashito's shift from striker to full-back, the development of his "full-pitch perspective" and his relationship with coach Fukuda. Skipping the first season would lose the foundation for both the tactical decisions and the character's growth.

OtaHub's take

Changing studios midway through a series is always a risky decision, but Aoashi has one point in its favor: series scriptwriter Masahiro Yokotani is the same person as before, so the risk of a shift in storytelling tone is lower than if the entire creative team had been replaced. In OtaHub's view, the real test for TMS Entertainment is not drawing well, but keeping the way season 1 illustrated tactical thinking, off-the-ball movement and the space between lines, which is what makes Aoashi different from sports anime that focus only on the moment of scoring. If cour 2 keeps that up at the intensity of a national-level youth tournament, this could be one of the most worthwhile sports anime of the Fall 2026 season.
