The Witcher 3 Remastered Review: Visual Upgrades That Don’t Fully Convince

The Witcher 3 Remastered upgrades lighting, shadows and reflections with ray tracing, but some scenes look less natural than the original. Our review and score.

Home › Gaming › The Witcher 3 Remastered Review: Visual Upgrades That Don’t Fully Convince
Gaming 2026-09-29 4 min read Yu
Key Takeaway
More than a decade after its release, CD Projekt Red brings The Witcher 3 back as a Remastered edition focused on improving visual quality and modernizing the experience. The new lighting, shadow and reflection systems got the most attention. However, the comparison video CD Projekt Red released before launch split the community: some players feel the original looks more natural and attractive in certain scenes, while others find the Remastered version more polished and complete.

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Remastered returns with the ambition of giving one of CD Projekt Red’s most famous open-world RPGs a more modern look. Lighting, shadows, reflections and many visual details have been upgraded, but these changes quickly sparked debate as some scenes in the new version were seen as less natural than the original.

A New Look for Geralt’s World

Judging by technical specs alone, The Witcher 3 Remastered has every reason to be called a significant upgrade. Ray tracing has been added to improve how light and shadows appear in the game world, while surface quality and environmental effects have also been reworked.

The problem is that these improvements don’t always have a positive effect.

Some scenes in the comparison video show the Remastered version with stronger lighting and reflections, but according to some players this also makes the image look less natural. The particle effects in the old version were also considered more eye-catching in some cases, while the new version’s textures were sometimes criticized as lacking detail.

This is exactly why the Remastered edition became a topic of debate right before launch. Instead of a consensus that the new version is superior in every way, CD Projekt Red faced comparisons between the old and new versions.

That said, it’s worth recognizing that a single comparison video can’t reflect the full experience of a game as large as The Witcher 3. Visual quality also depends on the platform, graphics settings and each area of the game. Some players still believe the Remastered version looks more complete and that the current controversy largely comes from the video not showing all of the new version’s upgrades.

New Technology Doesn’t Always Mean Better Visuals

This is perhaps the most important point when reviewing The Witcher 3 Remastered.

Ray tracing and new rendering technologies can make lighting more technically realistic, but “realistic” doesn’t always mean “prettier.”

The original Witcher 3 already had a clearly defined visual style. Colors, lighting and environmental effects helped create the distinctive feel of Velen, Novigrad and Skellige.

When these elements are adjusted, the visual experience changes too.

That’s why some players may prefer the old version even though the new one has more modern technology. It isn’t just a matter of hardware power; it’s also about how CD Projekt Red balances technical quality with artistic direction.

For a game that holds a special place in players’ hearts, changing familiar elements always carries risk.

The Witcher 3’s Value Isn’t Just About Graphics

If you judge The Witcher 3 Remastered only on its visuals, it’s easy to overlook the most important thing that makes the game so appealing.

The original is still remembered for its vast world, rich quest system and deep storytelling. Geralt doesn’t simply go from one quest to the next killing monsters. Players’ choices often have consequences, while many side quests have stories strong enough to stand on their own.

That’s also why The Witcher 3 still holds up even next to open-world RPGs released later.

The Remastered edition doesn’t need to change that formula. Its main job is to make this familiar experience better suited to modern hardware.

From that perspective, the improvements to lighting, effects and image quality are still valuable, especially for those stepping into Geralt’s world for the first time.

Is It Worth Coming Back?

For those who have never played The Witcher 3, the Remastered edition is still a notable choice. It’s a chance to experience one of CD Projekt Red’s standout open-world RPGs with upgraded visuals.

For those who have already finished the original, it’s more complicated.

If you’re expecting a complete graphical overhaul, the Remastered edition may not meet expectations in every scene. The debates before launch show that the gap between the two versions isn’t always clear.

But if your goal is to return to Geralt’s world with more modern visuals, the Remastered edition still has reasons to be played.

In particular, the fact that the changes don’t focus only on visuals but also aim to improve the overall experience makes this edition more valuable than a simple resolution bump.

Game Info

  • Title: The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Remastered

  • Genre: Action RPG, open world

  • Developer: CD Projekt Red

  • Publisher: CD Projekt Red

  • Release date: September 29, 2026

  • Platforms: PC, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2

  • Key technology: Ray tracing and graphical upgrades

  • Content: The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt and its expansions

Score: 8.5/10, Very Good

The Witcher 3 Remastered faces a difficult challenge: upgrading a game that holds a special place without losing the visual style players loved.

The new version shows that CD Projekt Red has invested significantly in modernizing image quality, but the debate over lighting, reflections and textures shows that not every change produces convincing results.

Even so, The Witcher 3’s core value remains intact. A vast world, carefully crafted quests and Geralt’s journey are still enough to keep players hooked.

The Witcher 3 Remastered isn’t a revolution, but it’s a notable return for Geralt — especially for those who want to relive this adventure on modern hardware.
