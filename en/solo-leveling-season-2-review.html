Solo Leveling Season 2 Review: Peak Action, Rushed Storytelling

Season 2 of the Solo Leveling anime won Best Action and Best Animation for its visuals and fights, but squeezing roughly 65 manhwa chapters into 13 episodes costs it depth in story and supporting characters.

Home › Anime › Solo Leveling Season 2 Review: Peak Action, Rushed Storytelling
Anime 2026-10-03 7 min read OtaHub Editorial
Quick verdict
Solo Leveling Season 2 is worth watching for its animation and fights, less so for its storytelling. A-1 Pictures packs roughly 65 manhwa chapters into 13 episodes, so the pace is fast, supporting characters are thin and many quiet beats from the source are cut. OtaHub score: 7.0/10, Decent.

Solo Leveling Season 2 -Arise from the Shadow- is A-1 Pictures' next anime season, 13 episodes that aired from January 5 to March 30, 2025. According to reading guides from CBR and ComicBook.com, it adapts roughly chapters 46 to 110 of the manhwa, taking Jin-woo from the Red Gate to the Jeju Island raid. Audiences and awards agree it looks superb: the 2026 Crunchyroll Anime Awards gave it both Best Action and Best Animation. OtaHub still scores it 7.0 for one concrete reason: Solo Leveling Season 2 tells about 65 chapters in 13 episodes, and the story pays for that speed.

What Season 2 is about: from the Red Gate to the Jeju raid

Season 1 built a survival story: a weak hunter has to understand the System to stay alive. Season 2 changes the question. Jin-woo is already strong, so the story asks how he uses that power without exposing his secret. It opens with the Red Gate, where an observation trip to a low-level raid turns into being trapped somewhere far more dangerous than planned. Later episodes revolve around curing his mother, his dealings with the big guilds and, at the peak, the Jeju Island raid, where Korean and Japanese hunters face an enemy beyond all forecasts.

On paper this is a good structure: three clear escalations and a large battle to close the season. The problem is not the outline but how much time each step gets, as the next sections explain.

Animation and action: why Season 2 won Best Animation

This is the strongest part and the least disputed. Motion in the fights is fast, camera angles keep shifting, and the shadow and blue-light effects around Jin-woo are as recognisable as they were in Season 1. At the 2026 Crunchyroll Anime Awards (held May 24, 2026 in Tokyo) the season won Best Action and Best Animation. Anime Corner's Winter 2025 poll also had it first for Best Action (25.81% of votes), Best Adventure and Best Fantasy.

The music keeps pace. Hiroyuki Sawano, who scored both seasons, was nominated for Best Score, and LiSA's opening "ReawakeR" was nominated for Best Anime Song, though both categories went to other films. If you simply want well-staged fights, Season 2 delivers.

Pacing: 65 chapters in 13 episodes

Numbers explain most of the debate. Season 1 adapted about 45 chapters in 12 episodes, close to four chapters per episode. Season 2 takes chapters 46 to 110, about 65 chapters in 13 episodes, roughly five per episode. The fights still run long enough to satisfy, while the build-up around them gets trimmed.

ComicBook.com lists what the finale dropped or compressed, including the Ant Queen shadow extraction, scenes with the Federal Bureau of Hunters directors and Thomas Andre's introduction. Rotten Tomatoes audience reviews also say the supporting cast is not memorable and many subplots feel underdeveloped. DoubleSama sees it the other way: the faster pace gave him more action and less downtime, and he scored the season 8/10.

Production and music

The team is nearly unchanged from Season 1: Shunsuke Nakashige directs, Noboru Kimura handles series composition, Tomoko Sudo designs the characters, Taito Ban voices Jin-woo and Reina Ueda voices Cha Hae-in. That stability explains why the look did not change. It also suggests the pacing was an editorial choice, not a staffing shortfall.

OtaHub's take: why 7.0 sits below the community score

Audience scores are high: 8.50 on MyAnimeList and 84/100 on AniList. OtaHub's 7.0 is clearly lower, and that is deliberate. The OtaHub scale gives 25% to story and content and 20% to lasting value, so a season that is superb at action but thin on character gets pulled down on those two criteria. Season 2 gives Jin-woo plenty of fights to win and less time to show what those wins cost him.

There is no single right answer. If you watch anime mainly for fights and animation, Season 2 deserves an 8 or higher. If you have read the manhwa, or want supporting characters room to breathe, 7.0 sits closer to the experience.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Animation and fight staging at the level of a Best Animation and Best Action winner.
  • Hiroyuki Sawano's score and LiSA's opening keep the big fights energised.
  • Fast, low-downtime pacing suits viewers who want continuous action.
  • The Season 1 staff returns, so the visuals stay consistent.

Weaknesses

  • About 65 chapters in 13 episodes, with many scenes cut or trimmed.
  • Supporting characters get little room and leave little impression.
  • A more serious tone with less of the manhwa's humour.
  • Few reasons to rewatch beyond the fights.

What critics say

On Rotten Tomatoes (read October 3, 2026), Season 2 sits at 100% from critics (5 reviews) and 80% from audiences (250+ ratings). Metacritic does not have enough reviews for a Metascore, but it lists Collider (Michael Thomas) at 80/100, saying the series is still very fun despite minor issues, and IGN (Rafael Motamayor) at 70/100 for the premiere, praising the action while noting its faults. Metacritic has only those two, and IGN covers just the first episode, so neither represents the whole season.

On the audience side, Anime Corner ranked Season 2 third for Winter 2025 with a 5.32% overall score, behind Re:ZERO Season 3 and The Apothecary Diaries Season 2. Kakao Entertainment said on May 26, 2025 that it was Crunchyroll's most popular series of Q1 2025. All of this leans more positive than OtaHub's 7.0; the gap comes from scoring by criteria, not from OtaHub finding the show poor.

Who should watch Solo Leveling Season 2?

Watch it if you like fantasy action, have seen Season 1 and want to see Jin-woo grow through carefully staged fights. Manhwa readers still have a reason to watch to compare the animation, though they will notice what was trimmed.

Think twice if you value supporting characters, a slower tempo and everyday scenes between fights. The show moves fast, and most of its emotional weight comes from battles rather than relationships.

Frequently asked questions

How many episodes does Solo Leveling Season 2 have, and when did it air?

Season 2 (Arise from the Shadow) has 13 episodes and aired in Japan from January 5 to March 30, 2025. Episodes are numbered 13 to 25 because Season 1 had 12.

Which manhwa chapters does Solo Leveling Season 2 adapt?

According to reading guides from CBR and ComicBook.com, Season 2 runs from about chapter 46 to chapter 110, so readers who want to continue should start at chapter 111. The anime skips or trims several scenes in that stretch.

Where can I watch Solo Leveling Season 2?

Crunchyroll streams the series outside East Asia. In Southeast Asia, Wikipedia lists Medialink as a rights holder too, so check the Crunchyroll catalog in your own region before subscribing.

Do I need to watch Season 1 before Season 2?

Yes, it is recommended. Season 2 continues directly and spends little time recapping. If you are short on time, the ReAwakening compilation film recaps Season 1 and adds the first two Season 2 episodes.

Is there a Season 3 or another sequel yet?

OtaHub has not found an official air date for a Season 3. A theatrical sequel, Solo Leveling: Beyond the System, was announced at Anime Expo 2026 (July 3, 2026) from A-1 Pictures and director Tao Tajima, with no release date yet.
