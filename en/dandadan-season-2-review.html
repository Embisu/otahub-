Dan Da Dan Season 2 Review: Science SARU Enhances Unique Supernatural Duo

Science SARU returns with a more cinematic and focused structure than season 1, Rotten Tomatoes 100%. OtaHub comprehensively reviews season 2 of Dan Da Dan.

OtaHub › Reviews › Dan Da Dan Season 2
Reviews 2026-08-15 6 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Dan Da Dan Season 2 Continues the hilarious supernatural adventures of Momo and Okarun under the direction of Science SARU. With a 100% Rotten Tomatoes rating from critics and 90% from audiences, season 2 is considered more focused and coherent than season 1, while still retaining the characteristic madness of the original Yukinobu Tatsu.

After the explosive success of season 1 (one of the most talked about anime of 2024 thanks to its groundbreaking animation and unique premise combining demons with aliens), Dan Da Dan Season 2 return with the pressure of huge expectations. Happily, Science SARU convincingly passed the difficult test "season 2 syndrome".

More structured: less rambling, more focus

While Season 1 tended to spread out across multiple storylines introducing the world, Season 2 is clearly much more tightly structured. The story arcs are arranged at a rapid pace, focusing on exploring the emotional depth of Momo and Okarun rather than just superficial comedy-action elements. The "cinematic" structure, used to describe the way scenes are set up, camera movements are used, and the framing is like a theatrical film, has been cited by many critics as the biggest difference from Season 1.

Animation: Science SARU continues to be the benchmark

Science SARU (the studio behind Devilman Crybaby and Scott Pilgrim Takes Off) continues to assert its leading position in experimental animation in the industry. The supernatural fight sequences between characters and alien/demonic entities are handled with a bold visual style, seamlessly blending traditional 2D and digital effects, maintaining the unique visual "signature" that made season 1 famous.

Balancing comedy - horror - romance is still the core strength

What makes Dan Da Dan stand out among countless supernatural anime is its ability to seamlessly switch tones: a scene can be simultaneously terrifying, funny, and touching in just a few minutes. Season 2 continues to build on this strength, particularly in developing the Momo-Okarun relationship in a more subtle way, avoiding the clichéd romantic formula often seen.

  • Original work: Manga by Yukinobu Tatsu (Shueisha, Shounen Jump+)
