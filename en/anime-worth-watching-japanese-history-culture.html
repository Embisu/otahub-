Anime Worth Watching For Japanese History Fans

Four anime that make Japanese history and culture less dry: The Elusive Samurai, The Heike Story, Golden Kamuy and Samurai Champloo, from the Kamakura and Heian eras to Ainu culture and Edo.

Home › Anime › Anime Worth Watching For Japanese History Fans
Anime 2026-09-26 5 min read OtaHub Editorial
Key Takeaway
Four anime that make Japanese history and culture less dry: The Elusive Samurai, The Heike Story, Golden Kamuy and Samurai Champloo, from the Kamakura and Heian eras to Ainu culture and Edo.

Historical anime is not only about samurai and feudal battles. Many use a real period as a backdrop to tell stories about people, beliefs, music and daily life, so viewers do not need to know much history to follow them. Here are four anime worth watching for anyone who wants a gentler way into Japanese culture and history.

When History Gets Less Dry Thanks To Anime

The Elusive Samurai (Nige Jouzu no Wakagimi)

The setting is the final years of the Kamakura period, when the shogunate fell in 1333 at the hands of Ashikaga Takauji. Protagonist Hōjō Tokiyuki is a real historical figure, a young lord forced to flee after his clan is overthrown. He is accompanied by the priest Suwa Yorishige and gradually builds allies on the road back.

What makes it different is that Tokiyuki is not good at fighting but at running away. Yusei Matsui's manga ran in Weekly Shonen Jump from January 2021 to February 2026 with 26 volumes and over 6 million copies, and won the 69th Shogakukan Manga Award in 2024. The CloverWorks anime had a 12-episode season 1 from July to September 2024, and season 2 began in July 2026; Crunchyroll streams it in many regions.

The Heike Story (Heike Monogatari)

This is an adaptation of The Tale of the Heike, the Japanese epic recording the Genpei War (1180–1185) between the Taira and Minamoto clans, originally passed on orally by blind musicians playing the biwa. The anime is based on Hideo Furukawa's 2016 translation into modern Japanese.

The protagonist Biwa is an orphan girl with special abilities, taken in by the Taira clan, also called the Heike, after its leader witnesses her power. Through her eyes, viewers watch the clan's rise and fall, a theme drawn from the Buddhist philosophy of impermanence in the source. It was directed by Naoko Yamada at Science Saru, with script by Reiko Yoshida and music by Kensuke Ushio, streaming from September 16 to November 25, 2021 and airing on TV from January 13 to March 24, 2022 in 11 episodes.

From Ainu Culture To Edo Hip-Hop

Golden Kamuy

The setting is early 20th-century Hokkaido after the Russo-Japanese War. Saichi Sugimoto, a veteran of the war, sets out with Ainu girl Asirpa to find hidden gold, drawn from a tale of Ainu miners who supposedly dug up 20 kan of gold.

The treasure hunt gradually becomes a journey through Ainu culture via language, food and customs, with Hiroshi Nakagawa of Chiba University as language supervisor. It is also a story of war trauma and mental recovery, mixing action, humor, adventure and daily life. Satoru Noda's manga ran from August 2014 to April 2022 in 31 volumes with over 29 million copies, winning the 2016 Manga Taishō and the 2018 Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize Grand Prize; the anime was made by Geno Studio for its first three seasons (2018–2020) before Brain's Base took over, and a live-action film released in January 2024.

Samurai Champloo

The Edo period is reimagined very differently: Fuu saves the swordsmen Mugen and Jin from execution, then forces them to help find a samurai who smells of sunflowers. Director Shinichirō Watanabe, who made Cowboy Bebop, blends the historical setting with hip-hop aesthetics, with music by Nujabes, Fat Jon, Force of Nature and Tsutchie.

Studio Manglobe's 26-episode series aired from May 2004 to March 2005. It does not put historical accuracy first, prioritizing world, characters and the acceptance of death that the director treats as its central theme. The series holds an 89% rating on Rotten Tomatoes.

Where To Start?

For a gentle journey, start with The Elusive Samurai for its quick, easy pace. The Heike Story suits viewers who like slow, emotional storytelling; Golden Kamuy is for those who want adventure plus Ainu culture; and Samurai Champloo is for anyone who loves music and style.
