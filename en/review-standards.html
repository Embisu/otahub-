Review Standards

Every OtaHub review is scored on 4 independent criteria instead of a single gut-feeling number, so you can see exactly where a game, anime or manga is strong or weak, not just whether it is "good or bad".

Why multiple criteria

No work is simply "good" or "bad" in every respect: a game can play brilliantly but have a shallow story, and an anime can look stunning but drag in its pacing. Collapsing everything into one number hides that. That is why every OtaHub review comes with a detailed breakdown across the 4 criteria below, alongside an overall score for quick comparison.

4 scoring criteria
📖
Story & Content
Writing, plot, character depth and themes. For games, this also covers world-building and how the story is told through play.
Weight 25%
🎮
Experience
Gameplay and how smoothly the mechanics work for games; pacing, direction and voice acting for anime; story pacing and panel layout for manga.
Weight 30%
🎨
Visuals & Sound
Graphics, effects and music for games; animation quality, visual direction and OST for anime; art and print/digital quality for manga.
Weight 25%
💎
Lasting Value
Is it worth replaying, rewatching or rereading? Post-game or post-story content, and how much it stays with you after it ends.
Weight 20%

The overall score is calculated from the weights above. The editor may then adjust it by up to ±0.2 if their overall impression after finishing the work differs from the pure calculation, because a review is the judgement of a real person, not a mechanical formula.

Transparency & Ethics
🚫 No money for scores
OtaHub does not accept payment, disguised advertising or any form of sponsorship from publishers or producers in exchange for scores or favourable review content. Scores reflect the writer's actual experience.
🎮 We disclose how we got access
If a game or episode was provided by the publisher for review (early access, advance codes...), the article says so. If we bought or watched it ourselves, we say that too.
✍️ One writer, one perspective
Every review carries a named writer and reflects that editor's personal view after experiencing the work, not a "community average". You may disagree, and that is completely fine.
🔄 Updated when needed
For live-service games or long-running anime, scores may be updated when the content changes significantly (major patches, new seasons...). Important revisions are noted in the article.
