Frieren: Beyond Journey's End Season 2 Review: Quiet, Deep and Precise

Season 2 is shorter, slower and light on tournament-style climaxes, yet it keeps what made season 1 special: small stops on the road that teach Frieren more about people. Madhouse changed directors without breaking the rhythm.

Home › Anime › Frieren: Beyond Journey's End Season 2 Review: Quiet, Deep and Precise
Anime 2026-10-03 6 min read OtaHub Editorial
Quick verdict
Frieren Season 2 keeps the tone of season 1 despite a change of director. It chooses a slow pace, many small stops on the road and a few meticulously staged fights; the price is that ten episodes leave some threads underfed, and community scores sit below season 1. Watch season 1 first. OtaHub score: 9.6/10, Masterpiece.

Frieren: Beyond Journey's End Season 2 (Japanese title Sousou no Frieren) is the sequel produced by Madhouse, adapting the manga by Kanehito Yamada and Tsukasa Abe. The season has 10 episodes, aired on Nippon TV from January 16 to March 27, 2026 and streams internationally on Crunchyroll in most regions. Frieren, Fern and Stark continue north after the First-Class Mage Exam, at a different rhythm from season 1: fewer episodes, more small stops and a new director.

OtaHub wrote this review from production data, the airing schedule and verified reactions from critics and viewers, not by retelling anyone's personal viewing experience.

What season 2 is about: north, one stop at a time

Season 2 opens right after the First-Class Mage Exam as the party heads across the Northern Plateau toward Aureole. According to aggregated sources, it continues the manga from chapter 61, where season 1 stopped. Rather than one overarching villain, each episode is a stop: a village, someone met on the road, a memory of Himmel's party. The episode titles show the rhythm, from "Shall We Go, Then?" to "Logistics in the Northern Plateau".

Supporting characters get more room. First-Class Mages Genau and Methode are expanded considerably, according to But Why Tho, and Fern and Stark get more everyday moments together. Episodes 34 to 36 bring in Serie's demon-hunting request and the fight with Divine Revolte, a demon who wields swords with four arms; it is the season's most concentrated stretch of action. The finale, "A Beautiful Sight", returns to a familiar strength: a bridge built by the dwarf Gehen over about 200 years and a deposit of Holy Snow Crystals, where the party stops just to look.

What changed from season 1: a new director, the same core team

Tomoya Kitagawa, who directed episodes 2, 8 and 25 of season 1, replaced Keiichiro Saito in the director's chair; Saito still lends support. Character design passed to Takasemaru, Keisuke Kojima and Yuri Fujinaka. The series composition, music and main cast stayed the same. The season itself is much leaner: 10 episodes against season 1's 28 (September 29, 2023 to March 22, 2024).

OtaHub's read: keeping the screenwriter, the composer and the three leads is why season 2 doesn't drift in tone. A director change is normally the biggest risk of a sequel season; in the reviews OtaHub read (But Why Tho, InBetweenDrafts) it isn't raised as a problem.

Visuals and music

InBetweenDrafts (10/10, April 14, 2026) praises the hand-drawn look as timeless, especially in small details such as a character's gait or gestures. The review also compares Evan Call's score to film composers like Howard Shore and John Powell, with distinct leitmotifs for different emotions. The opening "lulu." by Mrs. GREEN APPLE and the ending "The Story of Us" by milet (who also sang season 1's ending) complete that atmosphere.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Keeps season 1's tone despite the new director, thanks to unchanged series composition, music and main cast.
  • Very high visual and musical craft: careful hand-drawn detail, Evan Call's leitmotifs, milet's ending.
  • Supporting characters such as Genau and Methode gain depth, and the Divine Revolte fight is solidly staged.
  • A finale that closes the season gently without forcing a climax.

Weaknesses

  • Only 10 episodes, and some threads feel short of room (But Why Tho).
  • Slow pace with few through-lines; Screen Rant argued early in the run that it was sluggish and added questionable material.
  • A detail added to Frieren and Himmel's past was judged by But Why Tho to fall short of the show's standard.
  • Season 1 is essentially required viewing, so it is a poor entry point for newcomers.

What critics and viewers say

On Rotten Tomatoes (read October 3, 2026), season 2 stands at 100% from 6 critic reviews and 90% from the audience; the consensus is that it keeps season 1's standard, though one comment says the story has reached a plateau and lacks the first season's impact. But Why Tho scored it 8.5/10 (March 28, 2026), saying it offers less than the previous season but remains excellent. InBetweenDrafts gave it 10/10 and Anime Ignite 9/10.

In Anime Corner's weekly poll, season 2 ranked first in weeks 6 and 7 of winter 2026, and before it aired it topped the most-anticipated list. Among viewers, AniList gives it 87/100 (top-rated of winter 2026) while MyAnimeList currently shows 8.84/10 (#34). One caveat: its MAL score was around 9.3 early in the year and fell as more votes arrived; season 1 is the one now sitting at #1.

OtaHub's take. A lower community score than season 1 shouldn't be read as a drop in quality. A 10-episode season that leans on flashbacks and has few tournament-style climaxes will always struggle to match the first season's element of surprise. OtaHub scores it 9.6 for execution and for telling a long journey without hurrying, which is still rare in anime today. That number sits above the range of the reviews quoted here, and it is a deliberate choice: OtaHub weighs craft over plot speed.

Who should watch Frieren: Beyond Journey's End Season 2?

Watch it if you have seen season 1 and enjoy slow, resonant storytelling where each stop adds a piece of Himmel and the old party. Fans of film-grade scores and hand-drawn visuals will find plenty to like.

Think twice if you need a constant action plot or haven't seen season 1. In that case start from episode 1 and move on to season 2 afterwards. Season 3 (the Golden Land arc) is expected from October 2027, so you have time to catch up.

FAQ

How many episodes does Frieren Season 2 have?

Season 2 has 10 episodes, aired from January 16 to March 27, 2026 at 23:00 JST on Fridays on Nippon TV. In the overall numbering that is episodes 29 to 38.

What is Frieren Season 2's MyAnimeList score?

On October 3, 2026 MyAnimeList showed 8.84/10 from 301,769 ratings, ranked #34 on the site. It sat around 9.3 early in the run (Anime Corner noted it on January 19, 2026) before settling lower as more votes came in.

Do I need to watch season 1 before season 2?

Yes, ideally. Season 2 picks up right after the First-Class Mage Exam and doesn't re-introduce the bonds between Frieren, Himmel, Fern and Stark.

When does Frieren Season 3 air?

Season 3 adapts the Golden Land arc and is expected from October 2027 on Nippon TV. The exact date, episode count and director haven't been fully announced.

Where can I watch Frieren Season 2?

Crunchyroll streams season 2 worldwide except Asia (India excluded); in Japan it is also on Netflix, U-NEXT and Prime Video. OtaHub could not confirm a service specific to Vietnam, so check the app you use directly.
