Death Stranding 2: On the Beach Review: Kojima Surpasses Himself

Kojima Productions improves on every aspect of the first game, with deeper combat, better stealth, and a more mature Sam Bridges. OtaHub comprehensively reviews Death Stranding 2: On the Beach.

OtaHub › Reviews › Death Stranding 2
Reviews 2026-08-15 9 minutes read OtaHub Editorial
Summary
Death Stranding 2: On the Beach brings Sam Bridges, living with BB Lou in temporary peace, back on his journey to reconnect a broken society, this time in Mexico and then Australia. With a Metascore of 90, this is one of the highest rated games of 2025, far surpassing the first part (82 points).

11 months after the events of Death Stranding, Sam Bridges thought he could live peacefully with BB Lou. But an offer from Fragile pulled him into Mexico, which led to the mammoth task: connecting mainland Australia to the chiral network. Death Stranding 2: On the Beach It's not just a sequel, it's a complete upgrade of the Kojima formula.

Combat and stealth: outstanding depth

The most significant improvement over the first game lies in the combat system. The original Death Stranding was often criticized for its monotonous combat, but the second installment adds an RPG-style skill system, more diverse weapons, and a significantly deeper stealth mechanism, transforming confrontations with BTs or MULES from daunting evasive situations into scenarios where players can proactively handle them in various ways.

Story: Kojima-style, rich in emotion

In terms of plot, Death Stranding 2 retains Hideo Kojima's unmistakable storytelling style, both surreal and rich in metaphors about human connection. The introduction of new characters alongside the familiar cast expands the worldview without losing the emotional focus: the journey of a man trying to mend a broken world, this time not only for his mission but also for his adopted daughter.

Compared to the first part

This is a point of slight controversy among critics: despite improvements in almost every aspect, some opinions suggest that the sequel still feels more \"familiar\" than groundbreaking, lacking the novelty and characteristic friction that made the first installment a huge success upon its release in 2019. This isn't a serious flaw, but it's worth noting for those expecting a second revolution.
